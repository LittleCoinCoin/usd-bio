### Caveats on the RCT that the summary row cannot carry

- **It randomised whole posters.** Type size, layout, content reduction and the assertion sentence are confounded. No individual rule in this canon can legitimately claim `[rct]` warrant — only the format as a bundle.
- **Multiplicity.** One significant subscale among several outcome measures is the pattern expected under a true null plus noise, unless that subscale was pre-registered as primary. It is not reported as such.
- **A 2-item measure has a severe reliability ceiling.**
- **Immediate post-test only.** There is **no retention evidence for the poster format anywhere in this corpus** — which is notable given that delayed retention is exactly where Assertion-Evidence slides show their advantage.
- **The point estimate on overall transfer (6.83 vs 7.33) is unlabelled by arm in the source summary.** Do not read "null" as "no cost" without checking which arm scored higher.

### Independence asymmetry — the most decision-relevant fact here

Morrison co-authors the field study, the eye-tracking pilot (published in his own outlet), and the dataviz commentary. **The only two independent studies in the corpus are Lewis 2025 (posters — essentially null) and Sripradith 2023 (AE slides — positive, replicated).**

This is not an accusation; it is a description of provenance that should redistribute confidence: **raise it for the flash-talk rules, lower it for the poster rules.**

## 2. What Morrison himself cites

His template deck's own "research that influenced this design" slide lists **four Nielsen Norman Group articles** — inverted pyramid (need-to-know > nice-to-know), plain language being read faster including by experts, interaction cost, and minimising cognitive load.

These are UX practitioner articles, not peer-reviewed studies. Morrison has said in interview that the design came from iterative user feedback (~70% → ~90% approval across revisions), not from testing hypotheses drawn from the literature. **This is not a criticism of the design — it is a correct description of its provenance**, and it means "the research says" claims about specific visual choices should be made carefully.

## 3. The theory applied by analogy

Real, well-replicated cognitive science — but developed on narrated animations and text-diagram integration, **not on posters, walk-by viewing, or conference halls**. The transfer is a reasonable design inference, not a tested result.

- **Pirolli & Card 1999**, information foraging — the "patch switching" model behind dwell-time layering.
- **Mayer & Moreno 2003**, nine ways to reduce cognitive load — the *weeding* and *pre-training* techniques the format leans on. The coherence principle is strong (23/23 experimental tests, median d=0.86) but for multimedia instruction.
- **Chandler & Sweller**, split-attention effect — justification for keeping related content together rather than splitting it across bars.
- **Mautone & Mayer 2001**, signalling — highlighting essential content improves retention *of that content*, which is consistent with the RCT's narrow positive result.

## 4. Where the format is measured to lose

Carried into the canon as hard rules, not footnotes.

1. **Details and rigor** — IMRAD wins 67%. Hence: methods and limitation are mandatory structure.
2. **Overall knowledge transfer** — the RCT is null. The defensible claim is narrow: it makes *one finding* land.
3. **No time saving** — it does not take less time to make.

## 5. Documented criticisms

- **Occlusion** (Faulkes): presenters and crowds block the centre; the demo video is filmed in an empty room.
- **QR codes go largely unscanned** (Faulkes): printed far more than used.
- **Content fragmentation** (Faulkes): splitting results across both bars forces the reader to "dance back and forth".
- **Middle dead-zone** (Faulkes): attendees cluster at the edges where the readable detail is, leaving the billboard unattended.
- **Discovery model may be wrong** (Faulkes): many attendees plan visits from the abstract book rather than wandering.
- **Still text-heavy in practice** (Cheu): "the text is smaller and smashed into the corners."
- **False sense of quality** (Data Soapbox): using the template is assumed to make the poster good.
- **Negative first reaction; poor fit for image-dense evidence** (MIT #evenbetterposter).
- **Wastes space on a central statement** (Trauth); **spread on a splashy video rather than evidence** (Rutherford).
- **Colour-coding sections is arbitrary and a CVD hazard** (Faulkes).

## 6. The flash-talk side is better supported

Alley's Assertion-Evidence has a genuine numeric checklist *and* better evidence than the poster format:

- **Garner & Alley** (Penn State): AE beat traditional slides on higher-order questions immediately, and on **all** question types at one-week delayed test.
- **Sripradith 2023**, *Shanlax Int. J. Education*, n=59 — independent replication in a different domain (EFL grammar); the AE group's delayed post-test **exceeded** its own immediate post-test, p=.043.
- Honest limitation: the original studies used a live presenter narrating both conditions, so delivery differences may be partly confounded with slide design.
