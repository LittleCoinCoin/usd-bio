The six themes were right. Closing the set was wrong.

Plate 01 shipped six validated themes and then made a mistake: it treated the list as the deliverable. A list of six cannot absorb an institute colour, a funder's mandated blue, or a second poster in the same session that must not look like the first. The deliverable was never the list — it was the validation. This plate keeps the validation and replaces the list with a generator.

The reference point is Adobe's colour wheel, which is the right interaction and the wrong output. It hands you five swatches with no roles, no lightness contract, and no idea what surface they will be printed on. A poster theme is nine named roles under a contrast contract and an ink ceiling. So this wheel keeps the harmony rules and the drag, and adds the only thing that makes the output usable: hue is the only dimension the author gets to choose.

Reads on

Everything below is computed live from the wheel state. The six themes from Plate 01 are no longer definitions — section 04 re-derives them from the generator and reports how far off it lands, per role, in ΔE.

01

What a wheel has to own before it can be trusted

Two rules make the difference between a palette generator and a theme generator. Both are unglamorous and both are load-bearing.

Rule one: the author owns hue, the system owns lightness. WCAG contrast is a function of relative luminance, which is very nearly a function of lightness alone. If an author can set lightness, contrast becomes something you discover after printing. So every role has a fixed lightness target in OKLCH, and the wheel can rotate hue freely underneath it — the seven contrast checks from Plate 01 hold by construction rather than by luck.

Rule two: the sheet has three jobs, not five swatches. A poster needs a neutral family (paper, field, ink, rule), a plate hue, and one accent hue. That is three hue slots. A triad gives three, a square four, compound four — the extra slots are not a bonus, they are the mechanism by which a poster ends up with two accents and no five-second layer. The generator therefore assigns slots to jobs explicitly and tells you what it discarded.

What this borrows and what it refuses

Borrowed from Adobe's wheel: the harmony rules, the draggable markers, the sense that one gesture moves a whole set. Refused: the free colour picker. There is no field on this page where you can type a lightness, because that is the field that produces unreadable posters. The escape hatch is a mandated hex — the generator will rotate the wheel to put that hue in a slot, and then re-derive legal lightnesses around it.

        03

        Seven rules, and what each one is for on a sheet read at three metres

        All seven built at the current base hue and temperament, so the comparison is like-for-like. The number that matters is not how pretty the strip is; it is how many hue slots you have to throw away to install it.

        The recommendation, stated plainly

        Complement for a claim that must win a hall; analogous or monochrome for evidence-dense work. Split complement is the useful middle when the plate should differ from the ink family. Triad, square and compound are included so that choosing them is a decision rather than an accident — each hands you a hue with no job attached, and the discipline of this system is that such a hue gets discarded, not spent.

        Consequence for Plate 01

        The six stop being definitions and become attested instances: named wheel states, kept in the guidelines because they have been proofed and because a default is worth having. Decision 3 of Plate 01 — “six themes, closed set” — is withdrawn. What replaces it is narrower and stronger: any palette may be generated; only generated palettes may be installed.

        05

        What the generator refuses to emit

        An open generator is only safe if its refusals are explicit. These are checked on every state; a refusal is reported in the warnings panel and blocks the export stamp.

        One check from Plate 01 is deliberately not in this list: there is no test that a palette is beautiful, distinctive, or appropriate to the work. That judgement is the author's, and the wheel exists to make it cheap to exercise. The system's claim is narrower — whatever you choose will be legible, printable, and CVD-survivable.

        06

        What this plate asks you to decide

        Four decisions, one of which reverses a decision from Plate 01.

        1. Open the theme layer. Replace the closed set of six with the generator plus six attested defaults. This is the reversal; everything else follows from it.
        2. Hue is authored, lightness is not. The recipe's L targets become canon, and tokens.css gains a comment saying so. If you would rather authors could set lightness with a warning, say now — it is a one-line change here and an unfixable class of bad poster downstream.
        3. Three hue slots is the ceiling. Rules that emit four remain available but must discard. The alternative is to remove square and compound from the rule list entirely; I have kept them so the discard is visible, but I would not defend that hard.
        4. The refusal set in section 05. Especially the two numbers I invented: ΔE ≥ 14 against the categorical scale, and ΔE ≥ 10 for dichromacy. They are plausible and untested. One afternoon with a colour-blind colleague and a test print would let us replace both with something earned.

        This plate does not decide

        • The categorical data scale — still Okabe-Ito, still fixed (Plate 03, chart kit)
        • Type, geometry, or the zone proportions
        • Whether the contained plate stays at 22% of the sheet
        • How a theme is chosen for a given piece of work — that is a guidelines question, not an engine one

        Known soft spots

        • Chroma is one global slider; a palette wanting a quiet plate and a loud accent cannot say so
        • The repair pass moves lightness only, so a hue with little available chroma quietly desaturates instead
        • Ink coverage here is the reference schedule, not a measured layout — the live meters measure, the rule cards estimate
        • Dichromacy is modelled at full severity only, which is stricter than most real vision
