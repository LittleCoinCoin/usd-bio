Studio plate 03b · kit refactor · v3

Ink is a dial, not a constant.

Lai & Morrison's argument is that the data-ink ratio has a per-figure optimum you find by comparison — three charts, pick the one that is just right. The v2 kits could render exactly one of those three. This plate is the refactor: a dose parameter on every form, a default derived from the dwell layer rather than a global constant, the familiar chart form restored as the default where v2 had gone Tufte-minimal, extra ink concentrated on the emphasised datum instead of spread evenly, and greyscale re-read as a data-ink diagnostic rather than only a colour-vision check.

Warrant. Every number here is [house]. The source is a literature commentary in the authors' own outlet, graded opinion-plus-secondary-synthesis in the evidence base. It licenses a procedure and a diagnostic; it cannot warrant a default, so the defaults below are argued, not cited.

1 · The three charts, rendered

One dataset, one form, three doses. lean keeps the axis and drops the grid, prints only the emphasised number. mid adds the grid, three ticks, every value. rich adds interior points, five ticks, a leader to the axis and one annotation sentence. Nothing here is a slider position — each dose is a coherent stance on the continuum.

2 · The dose comes from the dwell layer

How much non-data ink to spend depends on the medium and the reader — which, on this sheet, is exactly what data-dwell already declares. So the kits now read it: dwell:'5s' resolves to rich, '1min' to mid, '5min' to lean. No kit function in v2 read the dwell layer at all. The methods kit inverts the mapping on purpose — a protocol figure earns more labelling the longer someone reads it.

3 · Familiarity is an axis the kit was not modelling

The v2 distribution() drew points and a mean bar and no box — Tufte's minimal boxplot, which is precisely the unfamiliar-form failure the commentary is about. Less ink, and slower to read, because the reader has to work out what they are looking at before they can look at it. The box is back as the default; the strip is opt-in; and 'both' is the honest maximum — familiar frame, real points, nothing hidden.

4 · Greyscale, re-read as a data-ink diagnostic

The greyscale proof already existed as a conservative sufficiency test for colour-vision deficiency and the mono laser printer. 5 · Claim graphics — the ring is familiar, not accurate

A ring gauge is instantly recognised and badly read: arc length against a circle is the pie problem in a thinner coat. The counted grid is just as familiar, countable, higher-ink, and more memorable — because the reader does the counting. Both stay in the kit; the grid is the one to reach for when the proportion itself is the finding.

To land in the repo

The two files beside this one are drop-in replacements for studio/chart-kit.js and studio/methods-kit.js — same exports, same signatures, v2 call sites keep working because the old scale boolean now resolves to the lean and mid doses. Three guideline edits follow from them: §7.2 gains the three-dose procedure instead of the phrase "aim for a middle data-ink ratio"; §7 gains a familiarity rule — prefer the conventional form unless the unconventional one is demonstrably clearer; and the greyscale row of the proof table gains its second reading. The pictogram kit and the diagram grammars need nothing: the commentary is about data graphics, and pictograms are non-data ink by definition, which it grants rather than governs.
